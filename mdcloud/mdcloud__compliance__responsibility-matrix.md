### [**Who is responsible for what in MetaDefender Cloud**](/docs/mdcloud/compliance/responsibility-matrix#who-is-responsible-for-what-in-metadefender-cloud)

MetaDefender Cloud is a shared service that OPSWAT runs on Amazon Web Services (AWS). Some decisions are yours, some are OPSWAT's, and the physical infrastructure is AWS's. This page shows which is which, so you know what you have to set up and what you can rely on us for.

| Area | You | OPSWAT | AWS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Private processing** | Private processing is off by default. You choose per scan whether to use it. With private processing, your file is deleted after it is analysed, and the scan results are available only to the owner of the API key, or to the organisation the owner is part of. Your sample sharing setting then no longer matters: the file is not shared with anyone. To have private processing enforced for all your scans, raise a ticket with OPSWAT Support (paying customers). | Without private processing, OPSWAT keeps your file and makes its results visible to other users of MetaDefender Cloud. With private processing, OPSWAT deletes your file after analysis, limits the results to the owner of the API key or their organisation, and does not share the file. | n/a |
| **2. Where your files are processed** | MetaDefender Cloud sends your file to the region you are in. If OPSWAT has no deployment in your region, your requests are routed to US West. To choose a region yourself, send your requests to that region's endpoint, for example [api-prod-eucentral1.metadefender.com](http://api-prod-eucentral1.metadefender.com) for Germany. The regional endpoints are listed on the Locations page. | Your files are processed and stored in the region you send them to and are not moved to another region. | AWS operates the data centres in each region. |
| **3. Sample sharing** | Sample sharing is enabled by default. If you keep it enabled, OPSWAT may share your file with the malware research community or its internal malware research team to investigate it further, in particular when the file is considered a false positive or potential outbreak malware. If you are a paying customer, you can disable sample sharing for a scan; the file is then deleted immediately once the analysis is done. If you use private processing, your file is never shared, whatever this setting says. | OPSWAT shares a file only while sample sharing is enabled for it and private processing is not used. | n/a |
| **4. Multi-factor authentication** | Nothing to turn on: multi-factor authentication is required for every user. If your organisation signs in through its own identity provider, OPSWAT's multi-factor authentication is disabled for those users, and enforcing it is your identity provider's job. | Since Jun 17, 2026 OPSWAT requires multi-factor authentication for all users, except those who sign in through their organisation's own identity provider. | n/a |
| **5. API keys** | You keep your API keys secret and ask for rotation or revocation when you no longer need a key, when the person who created it leaves, or when you suspect it has been exposed. Keys do not expire, so rotation is on your schedule. | OPSWAT rotates or revokes a key at your request through Support: it verifies that you are authorised, revokes the key and, for a rotation, delivers the replacement through a secure channel. To confirm a revoked key no longer works, call the API key information endpoint with it; it returns 401 Unauthorized. If you remove a user from your organization, their key keeps access to the results it submitted until you ask for it to be revoked. Request revocation whenever you remove a user. | n/a |
| **6. Users and roles** | Your administrator adds and removes users and gives each one a role. MetaDefender Cloud has three: Administrator, who can add other users, create sub-organisations and set their limits, change your security policies, scan files and view scan results; User, who can scan files and view scan results; and Analyst, who can only view scan results. A person must be registered with OPSWAT before you can add them. If you manage users in your own identity provider, disabling them there does not remove them from MetaDefender Cloud: you also remove them here and request revocation of their keys. | OPSWAT provides the Administrator, User and Analyst roles, described in , and applies them to every request. | n/a |
| **7. Single sign-on, IP allowlisting, mutual TLS** | IP allowlisting: your administrator adds the IP ranges allowed to call the API for your organisation, under Security Rules (availability depends on your licence). Single sign-on: you open a support case and provide your identity provider's SAML metadata and attributes; once enabled, OPSWAT's multi-factor authentication is disabled for your users. Mutual TLS (enterprise licence): you send OPSWAT your TLS certificate, with the whole chain of trust if it comes from your own certificate authority, and then include your certificate and private key in every request to the dedicated endpoint OPSWAT gives you. | OPSWAT enforces the IP ranges you set, confirms and enables your single sign-on integration, and, for mutual TLS, loads your certificate and sets up a dedicated endpoint for your organisation that accepts only requests carrying your certificate and private key. | n/a |
| **8. When your contract ends** | You ask OPSWAT Support to close your account through the Data Subject Request form, and export the data you want to keep before your request is processed. You can export your scan history, scan results and sanitized files from the last 24 hours, in JSON format, through the API. | OPSWAT deletes your files, scan results, scan history, private file hashes and organisation records from the live service and from backups in accordance with applicable laws, within the period that applies to your country or state, and tells you when deletion is complete. If you do not ask for deletion, OPSWAT keeps your files, scan results and scan history for up to 10 years from the date they were submitted, then deletes them. | AWS disposes of the physical storage media. |
| **9. Secure configuration and vulnerabilities** | You are responsible for the secure configuration of what you control: the settings in rows 4 to 7 (multi-factor authentication, API keys, users and roles, IP allowlisting, single sign-on, mutual TLS) and the systems and integrations that call MetaDefender Cloud. If you find a security vulnerability in MetaDefender Cloud, report it through the OPSWAT Vulnerability Program: submit it with the report form in My OPSWAT Portal ([opswat.com/security](https://www.opswat.com/security)), or write to psirt@opswat.com with questions about the program. Give OPSWAT a reasonable opportunity to validate and fix the issue before you publish details. | OPSWAT continuously monitors the configuration of its cloud infrastructure against its security policy with a cloud security posture management tool (Wiz) and remediates the findings. OPSWAT validates and triages every report received through the Vulnerability Program and fixes confirmed vulnerabilities within timeframes set by their severity. OPSWAT publishes your configuration responsibilities and the guidance for each setting in the product docs. OPSWAT publishes a security advisory when a vulnerability requires action from you, and notifies affected customers directly if their data was involved. OPSWAT maintains and patches the operating systems, containers and software the service runs on, and replaces components before their vendor support ends, as set in its End of Life Policy. | AWS secures and patches the underlying infrastructure: the physical hosts, the hypervisor and the network. |
| **10. Availability and continuity** | Your availability and recovery commitments depend on your licence and are published on the [licensing page](https://metadefender.com/licensing): an availability of 99.5% (Standard and Professional) or 99.9% (Enterprise), a response-time commitment per licence, and a recovery time objective and recovery point objective of 24 hours (Standard), 12 hours (Professional) or 4 hours (Enterprise). Check live status and past availability at [trust.opswat.com](https://trust.opswat.com/), where OPSWAT announces incidents and maintenance. You choose the regional endpoint you send requests to (row 2). | OPSWAT runs each region across several availability zones, with automatic scaling, so that the loss of one zone does not take the service down. If the primary US region fails, OPSWAT routes traffic to its secondary US region; for the other regions OPSWAT rebuilds the service from infrastructure code. Your data is replicated across zones and backed up daily. OPSWAT tests its disaster recovery once a year and provides the test report on request. Site reliability engineers monitor the service around the clock, following the sun across three locations, and OPSWAT communicates incidents on trust.opswat.com. | AWS operates the regions and availability zones and commits to its own service-level agreements for the infrastructure services OPSWAT builds on (compute, storage, load balancing, container orchestration, caching, messaging), published at [aws.amazon.com/legal/service-level-agreements](https://aws.amazon.com/legal/service-level-agreements/). |
